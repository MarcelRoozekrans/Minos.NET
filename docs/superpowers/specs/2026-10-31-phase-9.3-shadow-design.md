# Phase 9.3 Design — `Shadow mode and its report`

**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Phase:** `9.3`
**Surface:** `Backend`
**Stage:** phase (one phase, implementation-ready)
**Milestone design:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-milestone-9-design.md`

## Goal

An app that records its decisions can run a candidate provider, such as a local decision server, beside its primary
one in the background, without changing the primary's result or latency. It can then read a report that says how often
the two agree and how well each is calibrated against the outcomes the app confirmed. This is the evidence an app needs
before it switches provider in configuration.

Daedalus.NET is the first workload. Its requirements bind this phase: shadow mode is off by default and stays off the
decision's path; it is fire and forget, outside the caller's budget; its failures are invisible to the caller; a paid
candidate has a daily cap; nothing of the state reaches a log or a trace; it is AOT clean.

## What the milestone design already settled

- Shadow mode builds on the recorder: a shadowed decision is a record with the candidate's answers. It is a second
  opt-in, on top of recording.
- The candidate runs in the background through a bounded queue that drops when full.
- The daily cap is a count of calls per candidate, kept in memory for each process, with the day boundary in UTC. Over
  the cap the candidate is skipped and the skip is counted in telemetry. A cap that survives restarts is out of scope.
- The report gives per-key agreement, a confusion table of primary against candidate for Score and Choice, and the
  calibration of both the primary and the candidate against confirmed outcomes, as Markdown and JSON. It reuses
  `Minos.NET.Evaluation`'s metrics and output formats, and `minos` gains a command that builds it.

## Decisions (my calls; the maintainer asked to skip the questions on 2026-10-31)

1. **The record is written twice, and the sink interface does not change.**
   - The recording stage writes the primary's record as soon as the primary returns, exactly as it does today. When
     the candidate finishes, the recorder writes the same record again with its `Shadow` part filled in.
   - Both shipped sinks and `DecisionRecordFile`'s reader already treat a later record with the same decision id as
     replacing the earlier one, keeping its place and its outcomes. So `IDecisionRecordSink` (public since 1.3.0) gains
     no member, and a sink stays four methods.
   - Rejected: holding the record in memory until the candidate finishes. That delays every shadowed record by the
     candidate's latency (about 6.5 s for the local model) and loses the primary's record on a crash. It also lets an
     outcome reach the sink before its record, which breaks a database sink with a foreign key.
   - Rejected: a new sink method or a new `kind` of line for candidate answers. That is a breaking interface change or a
     second optional interface every custom sink must learn.
   - **What a custom sink must now do:** when shadow mode is on, `WriteAsync` can be called a second time for a
     decision id. It replaces the stored record and keeps its outcomes, as an upsert. The sink contract in
     `docs/recording.md` gains that rule. A sink that rejects the second write logs event 1010 and loses only the
     candidate's answers, never the primary's record.
2. **An erasure is never undone by a late candidate.** The candidate's write goes through the recorder's one queue. The
   recorder's writer remembers each decision whose candidate is still running, with its erasure key. An erasure, by key
   or by decision id, that it handles before the candidate's write marks that decision erased, and the candidate's write
   is then discarded and counted as dropped. Without this, the second write would come after the erasure line and the
   reader would keep it as a new decision.
3. **Shadow mode is part of the recording stage, configured with `ShadowOptions`.**
   - `UseRecording(recorder, shadow)` and a `RecordingDecisionClient` constructor overload take a `ShadowOptions`. The
     stage is the one that knows the decision id and the redacted state, so a separate stage would need a hidden channel
     to reach them.
   - `ShadowOptions` holds:
     - the candidate `IDecisionClient`, required;
     - `DailyCap`, required with no default: a paid candidate needs a cap, and only the app knows which candidates are
       paid. `int.MaxValue` is the explicit "no cap";
     - `Timeout`, default 30 s;
     - `MaxConcurrent`, the bounded queue of running candidates, default 4;
     - `Model`, the model to request from the candidate, default `null`, which is the candidate's default model.
       Never the primary's `request.Model`, which names a model of another provider.
   - The stage does not dispose the candidate client. It is the app's, like the recorder.
4. **When the candidate runs.** Only for a decision that the stage recorded, on its first attempt, and whose primary
   succeeded and was not a cache hit.
   - A failed primary gives nothing to agree with. A cache hit repeats a decision already shadowed.
   - A decision whose record the redactor dropped is not shadowed: no record means nowhere to put the answers, and the
     app said not to keep it.
   - A record that was dropped because the queue was full is not shadowed either.
   - Then the cap is checked, then `MaxConcurrent`. Each skip is counted with its reason.
5. **What the candidate is sent.** The same question set, state and images as the primary, unredacted: it is a
   decision, not a record. The candidate's call starts with `ExecutionContext` flow suppressed. So the caller's
   `DecisionRecording` scope and its `Activity` do not flow into it, and a candidate with a telemetry stage starts root
   spans, never children of the caller's trace. Its cancellation token fires on `Timeout` or when the recorder is
   disposed, never with the caller's token.
6. **What the record holds of the candidate.** A `Shadow` part, `null` when the decision was not shadowed:
   - the candidate's provider, endpoint and default model, read from its `DecisionClientMetadata` as for the primary;
   - the requested and served model, the response id and the upstream provider;
   - latency and usage;
   - the answers, as `RecordedAnswer` values, or the error kind and refused keys. A candidate that throws is recorded
     with an error kind as settled below. One past `Timeout` is `Timeout`.
   - In the file it is a `shadow` member on the record line, in the same shape as the record's own members.
7. **The format stays at versions 1 and 2.** A reader before this phase skips the unknown `shadow` member and reads the
   primary's record, which is right for tools that know nothing of shadow mode, `minos export` among them. The version
   2 rule extends: a record is version 2 when any answer, the primary's or the candidate's, has a probability source
   other than the model. A chat client is a likely candidate.
8. **Telemetry.** A new counter, `minos.recording.shadows`, by `result`, defined below under the builder check. Logs carry counts and exception type names only, as in the recorder. The candidate's error message, which can quote
   the request, is never logged or recorded.
9. **The report reads the record file, not an exported dataset.** Agreement needs no outcome, and an export keeps only
   decisions with a confirmed outcome. `minos shadow-report --records <file> [--out <dir>] [--prices <file>]` writes
   `shadow-report.json` and `shadow-report.md`. The library entry point is `ShadowReport.CreateAsync` (or a synchronous
   `Create` over decisions already read), in `Minos.NET.Evaluation`, with `ToJson()` and `ToMarkdown()` like
   `CalibrationReport`.
10. **What the report holds.**
    - **Counts:** decisions read; decisions shadowed; candidate errors by kind; refusals on each side.
    - **Per question key**, for decisions where both answered the key:
      - agreement, the share of the same answer. A Noul answer counts as yes when its probability is at least 0.5;
        Choice and Score compare the chosen index;
      - for Score, the mean absolute level difference too;
      - for Score and Choice, the confusion table: primary in the rows, candidate in the columns, labelled by option
        key or level.

      A key is grouped by its key and question kind. Two definitions with the same key and kind but different options
      are separate groups, labelled by their options (my call; it keeps a table's rows and columns meaningful).
    - **Calibration, both sides,** against the confirmed outcomes, merged as 9.2's export merges them (latest `At` wins
      per key). It uses `Minos.NET.Evaluation`'s existing metrics: accuracy, ECE and reliability bins per kind. The
      items it counts are settled below, so the two sides are measured on the same items. The builder may build this on `CalibrationReport` internals or on the metrics calculator directly,
      without changing what `minos calibrate` reports.
    - **Latency and cost** of both sides, with `--prices` priced as in `minos calibrate`.
    - **Signal outcomes** counted per signal name, for decisions where the two sides disagreed and where they agreed.
      They never become expected answers.
    - **Probability sources:** the count of verbalized answers on each side, as `CalibrationReport` does.
    - **Nothing that identifies a decision:** no id, no erasure key, no state, checked with a sentinel test.
11. **Exit codes for `minos shadow-report`** follow `minos export`. `2` means no shadowed decision could be read:
    a missing file, a file whose lines are all problems, or no shadowed record. `1` means a failure after writing
    started. `130` means cancelled. On `1` and `130` partial outputs are deleted.

## Settled after the builder check (my calls)

These sharpen the decisions above. Where they differ, this section wins.

1. **Calibration uses the same items on both sides.** A question key of a decision counts toward calibration only when
   the decision was shadowed, both sides answered that key, and a confirmed outcome answered it with an answer that fits
   the question. Fit is checked by the same rule 9.2's export applies before its merge. Decisions with images are
   included: the report reruns nothing, so the export's image skip does not apply. Each section of the report states
   how many decisions and keys it covers, including a count of keys answered by the primary only.
2. **The cap counts started calls.** The cap check and the `MaxConcurrent` check reserve their slots in one atomic step
   each, and a busy skip gives its cap slot back. A candidate that errs or times out still used its slot.
3. **The `shadows` counter describes the second write.**
   - `written`: the sink accepted the second write.
   - `failed`: the sink threw on it, logged as event 1010 as today.
   - `dropped`: the queue was full, the decision was erased, or the recorder was disposed.
   - `skipped_cap` and `skipped_busy`: the candidate never started.

   A candidate that erred is still `written`: its error kind is in the record. The second write does not add to
   `minos.recording.records`, so that counter still counts decisions.
4. **The recorder owns the running shadows.** An internal registry in `DecisionRecorder` holds each running shadow's
   decision id, erasure key and cancellation source. It serves decision 2's erasure check and gives the candidates
   their disposal signal. `DisposeAsync` cancels the running candidates at once, and their writes are dropped. It then
   drains the queue as it does today, so dispose takes no longer than `FlushTimeout`.
5. **Error kinds of a throwing candidate.** An `OperationCanceledException` from the stage's own timeout is `Timeout`.
   An `HttpRequestException` is `Network`. Every other exception is `Server`. A cancellation from the recorder's
   disposal is not recorded; it is dropped.
6. **A recording candidate is rejected.** The `ShadowOptions` constructor throws `ArgumentException` when the candidate
   returns a recorder from `GetService<IDecisionRecorder>()`. Its own records would pollute the file with duplicates.
7. **The file grows.** A shadowed decision has two full record lines in a JSONL file, state included. That is accepted
   and stated in `docs/recording.md`, next to the note that the JSONL sink is for development and tests.
8. **Report size.**
   - Every group is listed, with its comparison count. The Markdown marks a group with fewer than 30 comparisons as
     small.
   - JSON confusion tables are sparse: a list of non-zero cells.
   - In Markdown, a table with up to 12 options or levels is printed in full. A larger one, such as the 77 intents of
     9.4's Banking77 set, is printed as its 20 most frequent disagreements, primary answer to candidate answer with
     the count.

## Approach

### Runtime: `Minos.NET.Recording`

- `ShadowOptions`, and the `Shadow` part of `DecisionRecord` with its public type (proposed name `ShadowResult`).
- `RecordingDecisionClient` gains an internal shadow runner. After the primary's record is queued, the runner checks the
  conditions in decision 4. The cap counter is a per-stage count with its UTC day, reset when the day changes, read
  through the recorder's `TimeProvider` so tests control the day. The concurrency count is an `Interlocked` counter. The
  runner then starts the candidate on the thread pool with flow suppressed and returns at once. The primary path pays
  for two counter checks and one work item, and the AOT smoke app's allocation gate measures it.
- On completion, the runner builds the record again with the `Shadow` part and hands it to a new internal recorder entry
  point that queues a shadow write. A full queue drops it; it never waits.
- `DecisionRecorder`'s writer tracks running shadows by decision id and erasure key, and applies decision 2.
- `RecordLine` and `RecordLineConverter` write and read the `shadow` member. `DecisionRecordFile.WriteRecordLine`
  writes it, so a custom sink storing lines gets it with no change.

### Offline: `Minos.NET.Evaluation` and `minos`

- `ShadowReport` and its parts, reading `RecordedDecision` values.
- The confirmed-outcome merge from 9.2 is reused, not copied. The builder may move it to an internal helper both
  use.
- `ShadowReportCommand` in `Minos.NET.Tool`, registered in `MinosCli`, built like `ExportCommand`.

## Tests

- **Definition of done: the primary is unchanged.** Each of these returns the primary's result, and its call completes
  before the candidate's, with the candidate controlled by a `TaskCompletionSource` and fake time:
  - a slow candidate that never completes;
  - a failing candidate, both one that returns an error and one that throws;
  - a candidate over its daily cap;
  - a candidate when `MaxConcurrent` are already running.

  The caller's cancellation does not cancel the candidate; the candidate's timeout does.
- **The record:** the second write replaces the first in both sinks and through the file reader, and outcomes added
  between the two writes survive. The candidate's error kind and `Timeout` are recorded. The cap resets at the UTC day
  boundary. The candidate is skipped for a failed, cached, retried, redaction-dropped and queue-dropped primary.
- **Erasure:** an erasure by key and one by decision id, handled before the candidate's write, leave no record in the
  sink or the file. The write is counted as dropped.
- **Privacy:** no log, metric tag, span or exception message holds the state, an id or a key, checked with a sentinel
  string, as in 9.1. The candidate's spans are not children of the caller's.
- **Format:** round trip of the `shadow` member; a pre-9.3 fixture file still reads; a version 2 rule for a candidate
  chat-client answer.
- **Report:** agreement, the Score level difference and the confusion tables on hand-built records; calibration of both
  sides on the same items; signals counted by agreement; JSON and Markdown snapshots; the sentinel test.
- **Tool:** `minos shadow-report` on a fixture writes both files and exits 0; exit codes 2, 1 and 130.
- **Integration:** the real recorder, `JsonlDecisionRecordSink` and a fake primary and candidate record decisions with
  confirmed outcomes; `minos shadow-report` reads the file.
- **Regression:** the full suite, the AOT smoke run and surface checks, pack validation and the docs tests.

## Open Questions

None blocking. The plan's API review settles the final type and member names.

## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow mode slows the primary path | It cannot be used in production | Only counter checks and one work item on the path. Tests with slow and failing candidates. The allocation gate in the AOT smoke app. |
| A custom sink inserts instead of upserting | It rejects the second write | The candidate's answers are lost, never the primary's record. The sink rule is in the contract docs, and a sample sink shows the upsert. |
| A late write resurrects an erased decision | A privacy failure | Decision 2, with a test for each erasure kind. |
| A hosted candidate runs up a bill | Unexpected cost | `DailyCap` is required and `MaxConcurrent` bounds bursts. |
| The two calibrations are measured on different items | A misleading comparison | Only decisions where both answered and an outcome confirmed the key count. |
